The Hidering network
is temporarily suspended

A 51% attack has been detected on the v1.x chain. The public mainnet is frozen, and a coordinated hard fork v2.0.0 is being prepared to reset the network on secure foundations.

▸ Incident report — 16 May 2026

On 16 May 2026, at approximately block height ≈ 3,577, a 51% attack was observed on the v1.x chain of the Hidering network. An attacker who temporarily held a majority of the RandomX hashrate was able to reorganise recent blocks.

The limited hashrate distribution at this pre-public stage — Hidering had not yet reached its official launch — made this attack economically viable for a lone actor. No funds held by public users were affected, as the network was still in a controlled pre-launch phase.

The decision was made to abandon the v1.x chain and carry out a coordinated hard fork v2.0.0:

  • Network ID HRG\x01 → HRG\x02
  • Magic bytes 0x48524701 → 0x48524702
  • Version 1.0.2 → 2.0.0
  • Genesis unchanged (NUMS preserved)
  • v1.x chain abandoned at h ≈ 3,577

This cryptographic separation at the P2P handshake level guarantees that no v1.x peer can interact with the v2.0.0 network. A private mining phase controlled by the founding nodes precedes the public reopening, allowing time to consolidate hashrate distribution and prevent the incident from recurring.

Transparency note: the project's initial public communication advertised "zero premine." That promise was tied to the v1.x chain, which is now abandoned. The v2.0.0 private mining phase will be publicly documented at relaunch (volume mined, duration, distribution).
